# Singularity Dynasty

A subscription website for a dynasty fantasy football data-science business. Originally a single static `index.html` landing page (kept at repo root for reference); the live build is now a Next.js app in `web/`. This file is context for continuing the build in Claude Code.

## Brand
- **Name:** Singularity Dynasty
- **Theme:** space / black-hole aesthetic, with a secondary neon blue accent alongside the primary orange "event horizon" glow. A few minimalist football touches are woven in (the draft board grid mockup) but the site is not fully space-themed or fully football-themed — it's a deliberate blend.
- **Tagline / core concept:** "Where the data reaches its endpoint." The brand metaphor is gravity: scattered stats, trends, and matchups all collapse toward one clear number. The top pricing tier is literally named "Singularity" — the endpoint the whole brand points toward.
- **Voice:** confident, data-first, anti-"gut feel". Founder is a data analyst who got tired of vibes-based rankings.

## Win chance backtest (Oct 30 2026, founder: "a decision on the win chance: backtest it, or hide it")
**Verdict: keep it.** Two tests, both on the real 2026 season to week 7, both reproducible:
- **The disputed part, "points still to come" (`model/backtest_live_remaining_points.R`, run with a dump of `redraft_rankings` 2026):** 1,136 starter-level player-games (published median >= 5), PPR points rebuilt PLAY BY PLAY (nflfastR) and split at halftime (f = 0.5) and the end of the 3rd quarter (f = 0.25). **What a player did before the cut does not change what he does after it** -- regressing the points after the cut on f x median plus how far ahead of / behind his projection he already was gives a surplus coefficient of -0.005 (se 0.032) at halftime and -0.026 (se 0.020) at the end of the 3rd; the rule says 0, Sleeper's apparent "final stays near his projection" says -1. The f x median scale coefficient is 0.87 (se 0.07) at halftime and 1.00 (se 0.10) at f = 0.25. A rule where the final stays at his projection predicts the remaining points clearly worse (mean absolute error 4.97 vs the rule's 3.98 at halftime, 4.16 vs 2.97 at f = 0.25). Sanity checks: the rule's 10th / 90th limits are hit 7.1% / 11.6% of the time at halftime and 3.7% / 13.8% at f = 0.25 (a slightly wide left side and narrow right side late; not worth a change), and 45% of players land above f x median (the median sits below the mean of a skewed outcome). **So the 78% vs Sleeper's 35% disagreement in the readings below comes from Sleeper expecting less from a player who is already ahead, and the data say that expectation is wrong** -- with the caveat that Sleeper's real method is unknown (only the founder's readings of its projected finals were used) and that a player-game here is not a whole matchup.
- **The whole number before kickoff (`web/scripts/backtest-pregame-winchance.mjs`):** the 5 saved leagues' real matchups, weeks 1-7, 210 head-to-heads replayed through `lib/winChance.js` with the projections as published each week. Brier 0.196 (always-50% is 0.250), log loss 0.585 (0.693), picks the winner 69.5% of the time, calibration slope 0.94 (1.00 = calibrated; mildly too confident), and the reliability bins sit close to the diagonal (said 65-80% -> won 70.7%, said 80-100% -> won 82.9%; only the 0-20% bin is off, 20 matchups, said 13% -> won 5%). Limits: a few hundred matchups from a few leagues, our standard-PPR projections against each league's own scoring (extra noise the live site doesn't have), and the in-game win chance as a whole (not just its remaining-points part) has no snapshots to test against -- the Sunday board's percentages are therefore supported but not proven end to end.
- Nothing in the model was changed. If more leagues are ever saved, rerun the second script; the first needs `Rscript backtest_live_remaining_points.R <dump>` after dumping `redraft_rankings` to CSV (columns season, week, player_name, position, team, projection, floor, ceiling, notes).
